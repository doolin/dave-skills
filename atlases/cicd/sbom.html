Software bill of materials (SBOM)

A software bill of materials (glossary: SBOM) is a machine-readable list of the components in a piece of software and the dependencies between them. This page records its definition, its history, its formats, the pipeline stage at which each type is produced, and the NIST, STIG, and Department of Defense documents that refer to it. An SBOM is evidence toward a control, and each control also requires a process; §6 to §8 give the controls and state whose mapping each is.

§1

What an SBOM is

factkindsource
The federal definition: "the term 'Software Bill of Materials' or 'SBOM' means a formal record containing the details and supply chain relationships of various components used in building software. Software developers and vendors often create products by assembling existing open source and commercial software components. The SBOM enumerates these components in a product. It is analogous to a list of ingredients on food packaging."documentedE1 · Sec. 10(j)
The same paragraph lists three users: "those who develop or manufacture software, those who select or purchase software, and those who operate software." The builder keeps components current; the buyer does "vulnerability or license analysis"; the one running it can "quickly and easily determine whether they are at potential risk of a newly discovered vulnerability."documentedE1 · Sec. 10(j)
NTIA's working definition, two years earlier: "An SBOM is effectively a nested inventory, a list of ingredients that make up software components." CISA's SBOM page carries the sentence still, less the word "effectively".documentedN2 · §2; C2 · cisa.gov/sbom
The structure is hierarchical. "A piece of software can be represented as a hierarchical tree, made up of components that can, in turn, have subcomponents, and so on." Each component "should have its own SBOM listing their components".documentedN1 · IV
The minimum elements are three areas: data fields, automation support, and practices and processes. Automation support means "automatic generation and machine-readability"; the practices are "Frequency, Depth, Known Unknowns, Distribution and Delivery, Access Control, and Accommodation of Mistakes."documentedN1 · I, table

The seven data fields, with NTIA's description of each (N1 · IV, Data Fields).

data fielddescription, verbatim
Supplier Name"The name of an entity that creates, defines, and identifies components."
Component Name"Designation assigned to a unit of software defined by the original supplier."
Version of the Component"Identifier used by the supplier to specify a change in software from a previously identified version."
Other Unique Identifiers"Other identifiers that are used to identify a component, or serve as a look-up key for relevant databases." The examples given are CPE, SWID tags, and Package URL (PURL).
Dependency Relationship"Characterizing the relationship that an upstream component X is included in software Y."
Author of SBOM Data"The name of the entity that creates the SBOM data for this component." Not the author of the software: "just the source of the descriptive data."
Timestamp"Record of the date and time of the SBOM data assembly."
Author and supplier. The two differ when someone describes software they did not make. NTIA's 2019 paper gives an SBOM author four things to say about a component's own dependencies: Unknown (the default), Root (it has none), Partial, and Known. "Known unknowns" in the 2021 practices is the same idea: an SBOM says where its knowledge stops (N2 · §2.4.1; N1 · I).

§2

Where the notion came from

A bill of materials is a manufacturing document: the parts list for an assembly, with sub-assemblies listed under their parents.

whenfactkindsource
1964, 1975Material requirements planning, the method that computes what a factory must order from a product's bill of materials, is credited to Joseph Orlicky in 1964; his book Material Requirements Planning was published by McGraw-Hill in 1975. The article that says so gives no citation for the 1964 sentence.unverifiedW1 · History
—The NTIA paper refers to manufacturing practice for the nested structure: "Bills of materials for physical components often describe these relationships as a 'Multi-level BOM.'" Its scope statement begins "Learning from principles of supply chain management in other fields".documentedN2 · §2.4, §6.3
2010-02"specification drafting began in a work-group of FOSSBazaar under Linux Foundation that came to be called 'SPDX,'" and "was originally referred to as Package Facts." The first use of a standard component list for software was licence compliance; SPDX's stated aim is "to reduce redundant work by providing common formats for organizations and communities."documentedX1 · overview
2011-08"SPDX 1.0 specification handles packages."documentedX1 · overview
2014-12-04H.R. 5793, the "Cyber Supply Chain Management and Transparency Act of 2014", is introduced in the House by "Mr. Royce (for himself and Ms. Jenkins)". Under it, federal contracts would require "a comprehensive and confidentially supplied list, or a bill of materials, of each binary component" of the software, firmware, or product.documentedH1 · Sec. 2(a)(1)(A)
2014-12The bill was referred to the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform; the excerpt records no later action.unverifiedH1 note · congress.gov record
2018-03"OWASP CycloneDX v1.0 — First general-purpose, security-focused Bill of Materials standard supporting software and hardware components."documentedX2 · history
2018-07-19"the National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) convened a meeting of stakeholders from across multiple sectors to begin a discussion about software transparency". The meeting set up four task groups: framing, use cases, standards and formats, and a healthcare proof of concept between hospitals and medical device makers.documentedN2 · §6.1
2019-11-12The framing group's paper, Framing Software Component Transparency: Establishing a Common Software Bill of Material (SBOM), defines the baseline attributes that became the 2021 data fields.documentedN2 · paper
The paper's examples. The worked examples in the NTIA paper use invented components: Acme Application v1.1 includes Bob's Browser and Bingo Buffer, and Bob's Browser includes Carol's Compression Engine. A second supply chain uses Nancy's NanoPhone, Oscar's OpenLibrary, and Paul's Protocol 2012. The paper calls the case where every supplier publishes a complete SBOM "this state of transcendent SBOM utopia" (N2 · §2.4.1, §2.5, §4.4).

§5

Where in a pipeline an SBOM is made

The six types of the CISA working group's paper, in life-cycle order, each with its definition verbatim (C1 · Table 1). The paper says the list "is not intended to be tightly tied to the software lifecycle" and that one document may combine types.

1Design"SBOM of intended, planned software project or product with included components (some of which may not yet exist) for a new software artifact."
2Source"SBOM created directly from the development environment, source files, and included dependencies used to build an product artifact." "Typically generated from software composition analysis (SCA) tooling, with manual clarifications."
3Build"SBOM generated as part of the process of building the software to create a releasable artifact (e.g., executable or package) from data such as source files, dependencies, built components, build process ephemeral data, and other SBOMs."
4Analyzed"SBOM generated through analysis of artifacts (e.g., executables, packages, containers, and virtual machine images) after its build." Also called a third-party SBOM.
5Deployed"SBOM provides an inventory of software that is present on a system."
6Runtime"SBOM generated through instrumenting the system running the software, to capture only components present in the system, as well as external call-outs or dynamically loaded components."
factkindsource
Generation during the build is recommended. NTIA, 2019: "SBOM information will ideally be generated as an integral part of the supplier's software build and packaging processes". The CISA paper lists as a benefit of the Build type "Increased trust by enabling signing of the SBOM and product artifact by the same build workflow", and as a limit that it "May be difficult to capture indirect and/or runtime dependencies."documentedN2 · §4.1;C1 · Table 2
Frequency. "A new SBOM should be created for every new release of a component." It "should be created or updated when information about included components changes, even if the components themselves have not changed." The SSDF says the same of provenance data: "Update the provenance data every time any of the software's components are updated."documentedN2 · §4.2; S1 · PS.3.2 Example 4
For open-source code pulled into a project, developers "should verify digital signatures, run vulnerability scans, check for recent updates on newly downloaded OSS's source-code packages, and generate an SBOM with dependency scanning on the first commit".documentedS4 · §3.2.1
A CI framework's security goals include "Support for infrastructures for generating the evidence (e.g., SBOM generators, Digital signature generators)". What an SBOM must contain is left to others: such artifacts are "continually evolving" and "beyond the scope of this document."documentedS4 · §5.1, §1.2
Integrity of the SBOM. The SSDF: "Protect the integrity of provenance data, and provide a way for recipients to verify provenance data integrity." NTIA, 2019: an SBOM system "must support the ability to cryptographically authenticate SBOM information", though signing "is optional".documentedS1 · PS.3.2 Example 3; N2 · §2.6.1
Limits by type. A Source SBOM "Can highlight components (which might have vulnerabilities) that never run or are compiled out in deployed code." An Analyzed SBOM "May be prone to omissions, errors, or approximations if the tool is unable to decompose or recognize the software components precisely."documentedC1 · Table 2

§8

DoD and Air Force continuous authorization

The three DoD CIO documents on continuous authorization: the 2022 memorandum, the 2024 implementation guide, and the 2024 evaluation criteria. The criteria are the document that requires an SBOM by name.

factkindsource
The memorandum "Continuous Authorization To Operate (cATO)", signed 2 February 2022 by David W. McKeown, DoD Senior Information Security Officer: "In order to achieve cATO, the Authorizing Official (AO) must be able to demonstrate three main competencies: On-going visibility of key cybersecurity activities inside of the system boundary with a robust continuous monitoring of RMF controls; the ability to conduct active cyber defense in order to respond to cyber threats in real time; and the adoption and use of an approved DevSecOps reference design."documentedD1 · p. 1
Its section "Secure Software Supply Chain": "In order to prevent any combination of human errors, supply chain interdictions, unintended code, and support the creation of a software bill of materials (SBOM), the adoption of an approved software platform and development pipeline(s) are critical." It is the memorandum's one mention of an SBOM.documentedD1 · p. 2
For cATO, "all security controls will need to be fed into a system level dashboard view", and "DoD CISO approved cATOs do not have an expiration date and will remain in effect as long as the required real time risk posture is maintained."documentedD1 · p. 1, p. 3
The Continuous Authorization to Operate (cATO) Evaluation Criteria, DevSecOps Use Case, 29 May 2024, lists what the DoD CISO considers in a cATO request. Under "3.0 Secure Software Supply Chain (SSSC) and DevSecOps", "3.1 Authorize the DevSecOps Platform", an item "Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)" requires: "Provide a SBOM for the DSOP with a statement of how it was developed"; "Provide an automated export of the SBOM for applications/products passing through the DSOP"; "Specify the SBOM format and how often it is generated"; "Maintain an archive of SBOMs for products passing through pipelines"; and "Explain how the SBOMs are analyzed. If a new cybersecurity vulnerability appears in the Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE®) system, explain how the organization applies it to the SBOMs".documentedD3 · App. D §3.1, p. 14
On format: "SBOM should be in one of the common formats": SPDX, SWID, CycloneDX. "However, the format has not yet been mandated. SBOM is currently undergoing regulatory action, Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA) Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) is the lead".documentedD3 · p. 14
The same section ties software composition analysis to the SBOM: "Software Composition Analysis to review artifacts to find open-source libraries included. This should be addressed in the creation of the SBOMs."documentedD3 · p. 15
The criteria evaluate supply chain processes against "cybersecurity supply chain risk management guidance in accordance with NIST SP 800-161r1", and the approval follows "CNSSI 1253, NIST SP 800-53 and DoDI 8510.01". A cATO also requires "a current ATO with no 'High' or 'Very High' unmitigated findings."documentedD3 · App. B, p. 8
The DevSecOps Continuous Authorization Implementation Guide, DoD CIO, March 2024, version 1.0, quotes the memorandum's SBOM sentence and relates the supply chain to the third competency. Its implementation advice under "Secure Software Supply Chain": "Create SBOMs for the DSOP and applications passing through it."documentedD2 · p. 2, p. 12
The guide's Appendix B, Table 2, lists cATO requirements as threshold (T, must be met) or objective (O). None uses the word SBOM. The nearest is SA04, threshold: "Perform security scanning: dependency analysis, static and dynamic application analysis, prioritizing method, and adjudication."documentedD2 · App. B, p. 16
The Air Force's Fast Track ATO, March 2019, let an authorizing official decide on a cybersecurity baseline, a penetration test, and continuous monitoring. Press accounts differ on the memorandum's date (18 or 22 March) and signer. Platform One followed it and is described as the Department of the Air Force's DevSecOps platform with a continuous authorization.unverifiedD5 · Nextgov

§11

Relationships

  • Provenance and attestation. An SBOM says what is in a release. A build attestation says how the release was made: SP 800-204D lists environment, process, and materials attestations signed by a part of the build "not under the control of the developers" (S4 · §5.1.1). The SSDF files both under provenance data (PS.3.1, PS.3.2). A later page.
  • VEX. Reads an SBOM's component list and adds, per vulnerability, whether the product is affected (§9). A later page.
  • Vulnerability scanning. Consumes an SBOM; the portfolio's bin/check-bundle-audit does the same job from lockfiles for Ruby alone (§10).
  • The component inventory. CM-8's inventory is of a system; an SBOM is of a release. A Deployed SBOM (§5) is where the two meet.
  • HBOM. The 2021 report set hardware aside as deserving "its own treatment"; M-26-05 points agencies to CISA's 2023 hardware bill of materials framework (N1 · III; O1 · p. 2).

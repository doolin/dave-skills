Atlases · CI/CD · audit events

Pipeline audit events

A pipeline audit event is the structured record a CI/CD job writes of what it did: which job ran, for which commit and run, when, and with what outcome. The sources call such a record an "audit record" or a "log entry". This page records the definitions, the pipeline stage at which the records are made, the NIST, STIG, Department of Defense, and federal records documents that ask for them or set their retention, and what the portfolio's pipelines write. An audit event is evidence toward a control; each control also requires a process (event selection, review, protection, retention), and §3 states whose mapping each control is.

§1

What an audit event is

factkindsource
The federal definition of logs: "the term 'logs' means records of the events occurring within an organization's systems and networks. Logs are composed of log entries, and each entry contains information related to a specific event that has occurred within a system or network."documentedE1 · Sec. 10(i)
The order's sentence is SP 800-92's, fifteen years earlier: "A log is a record of the events occurring within an organization's systems and networks. Logs are composed of log entries; each entry contains information related to a specific event that has occurred within a system or network." Log management is "the process for generating, transmitting, storing, analyzing, and disposing of computer security log data."documentedS7 · §2, p. 2-1
SP 800-53 defines the event: "An event is an observable occurrence in a system. The types of events that require logging are those events that are significant and relevant to the security of systems and the privacy of individuals."documentedS3 · AU-2; S2 · AU-2
What an audit record holds. AU-3: "Ensure that audit records contain information that establishes the following: a. What type of event occurred; b. When the event occurred; c. Where the event occurred; d. Source of the event; e. Outcome of the event; and f. Identity of any individuals, subjects, or objects/entities associated with the event." The discussion pairs each item with a field: "event descriptions (item a), time stamps (item b), source and destination addresses (item c), user or process identifiers (items d and f), success or fail indications (item e), and filenames involved (items a, c, e, and f)".documentedS3 · AU-3; S2 · AU-3
SP 800-92's account of an operating system's event entries: "each event is usually timestamped, and other supporting information could include event, status, and error codes; service name; and user or system account associated with an event."documentedS7 · §2.1.2, p. 2-4
The SSDF's term for what a tool leaves behind: "An artifact is 'a piece of evidence' [adapted from IR7692]. Evidence is 'grounds for belief or disbelief; data on which to base proof or to establish truth or falsehood' [SP800160]. Artifacts provide records of secure software development practices."documentedS1 · p. 7, note 6
The NSA and CISA guide states what a CI/CD audit log is for: "An audit log should provide clear information on who committed, reviewed, and deployed what, when, and where. If all previous measures fail, an audit log will at least help forensically reconstruct an incident post-compromise, so it can be quickly addressed".documentedK1 · p. 14
The term. "Audit event" is the portfolio's term; the skill that springboard's pipeline follows asks for "Structured JSON audit events" with "event, severity, timestamp, and context fields" (dave-skills · skills/cicd-golden-pipeline/SKILL.md · 96d1f33). The issuing documents' terms are "event" (the occurrence), "audit record" and "log entry" (the record of one), and "audit trail" or "log" (the collection). This page uses theirs where it quotes them.

§2

Where in a pipeline it is made

The sources place logging in every phase and tie the build's record to the attestation of the build. The one-record-per-job shape is the portfolio's (§8).

factkindsource
SP 800-204D lists "Audit and monitoring" among the "generic mitigation measures" that "are applicable to the entire SDLC but are particularly relevant to an SSC". Among baseline measures: "CI/CD processes should be audited regularly, and automation should be introduced wherever possible", and "There should be enhanced real-time monitoring and alerting mechanisms to detect suspicious activities in CI/CD servers, especially activities that might indicate the exfiltration of sensitive data or the tampering of builds."documentedS4 · §3.2, p. 11, §3.2.1, p. 12
In the secure build, a CI tool's commands are wrapped "with capabilities to gather evidence and ultimately create an evidence trail of the entire SDLC". The second type of evidence "consists of the hash of the final build artifact, files, libraries, and other materials used in the artifacts and all events. This is then signed by a trusted component of the build framework that is not under the control of the developers". The evidence "should be enabled by a process with a higher level of trust or isolation than the build itself to protect against tampering."documentedS4 · §5.1.1, p. 16
Threats to software update systems "primarily target the evidence generation process so as to erase the trail of updates and prevent the ability to determine whether the updates were legitimate or not."documentedS4 · §5.1.3, p. 18
DevSecOps Fundamentals: "It is crucial to monitor each step, or link, in the software supply chain across every segment of the CI/CD pipeline. This monitoring involves collecting and analyzing comprehensive metadata at each stage". "By aggregating logs, metrics, and traces from each step, teams can quickly diagnose issues, ensure compliance with security policies, and optimize the pipeline for efficiency."documentedD8 · pp. 13–14
The Activities & Tools guidebook files "Logging" under the Operate phase (Table 11) and "Log Analysis & auditing" and "Log auditing" under Monitor (Table 12), all REQUIRED and keyed to SSDF PO.3 tasks (§5). In the Build phase (Table 6), "Build configuration control and audit", REQUIRED, keyed to PS 3.2: "Track build results, SAST and dependency checking report".documentedD7 · Table 6, Table 11, Table 12
The ESF developers' guide, on the build pipeline: "All access to build pipeline systems should be logged. At minimum, log the MFA ID of the user authenticating access and the date and time." Service account logins "should be logged. These logs should include date and time and the origin of login."documentedK2 · p. 30
The ESF guide's Appendix D lists a "Build Log" artifact, used for "Attestation to the integrity of securely built products", "Attestation to no known critical errors/warnings", and "Attestation to use of tool-chain defenses (stack checking, ASLR, etc.)". Its checklist asks "Do you document approval and audit logs of build chain modifications?"documentedK2 · App. D, p. 44, checklist
NSA and CISA list "Keep audit logs" among the mitigations for a CI/CD environment compromised through its configuration, infrastructure-as-code, source, or dependencies (Scenario 3), beside "Integrate security scanning as part of the CI/CD pipeline".documentedK1 · p. 9

NIST: the SSDF tasks and the SP 800-53 controls

Each control below also requires a process (which events, how often reviewed, who is alerted, how long kept), so an audit event is evidence toward the control and does not meet it alone. The first table is NIST's mapping, from the SSDF's reference column. The second quotes the control text from the OSCAL catalog, release 5.2.0, linked to the page of the 2020 PDF; its "basis" column marks the rows that are this page's reading.

STIG

The Application Security and Development STIG, Version 6 Release 5 (DISA package U_ASD_V6R5_STIG.zip; a reader's copy at STIG Viewer), 286 rules. Its rules are written for an application's own logs, and a pipeline is not the application, so applying them to pipeline records is this page's reading. The rules below are the content, time, off-loading, protection, retention, and review rules; each is medium severity (CAT II). The CCI and its SP 800-53 Rev. 5 reference are as the STIG's CCI lines give them. The event-type rules (account actions, privileges, session ids, logons, data access) concern an application's users and are left out.

§9

Relationships

  • Evidence manifest (evidence-manifest.html). Springboard's manifest hashes the audit events of the seven jobs verify-evidence waits on and gives each the classes audit-event and standard (§8). Shared controls: AU-9(3), AU-11, SI-12.
  • Attestation (attestation.html). The attest job archives the uploaded events with every other artifact, hashes the archive, and records the hash in a signed Solana transaction. slacronym's run-record.json and clubstraylight.com's attestation.json are written by the attest scripts and are those repositories' nearest records to an audit event. SP 800-204D ties the two: the signed evidence covers "all events" (§2). Shared controls: AU-9(3), AU-10.
  • Timestamp (timestamp.html). An audit event's time comes from the runner's clock (AU-8); a time-stamp token comes from a third party. Springboard's attest job uploads timestamp.tsr beside the archive.
  • The checks that emit events: secrets-scan.html, test-results.html, vulnerability-scan.html, static-analysis.html (Checkstyle), sbom.html, oscal.html. Each job's event records that the job ran and its status; the job's report carries the findings.
  • Provenance (provenance.html). Provenance states how an artifact was built; an audit event states that a job ran. SP 800-204D's signed build evidence covers both the materials and "all events" (§2); the SSDF's provenance task and its mapping are on sbom.html §6.
  • Coverage (coverage.html). Springboard's manifest classes coverage, test results, and audit events alike as "standard", 90 days.

§10

Observed

  • Of springboard's thirteen jobs with steps across its two workflows, eight write and upload an audit event, three echo one to the job log only, and two (lint-frontend, pr-summary) have no emit step. Four of the eight files carry stage (2026-11-14).
  • verify-evidence writes its own event after the manifest, so the manifest holds seven events; the attest job, which downloads after the whole golden pipeline, gets all eight. This is a reading of the workflow, not of a run's artifacts.
  • The key event means the job in springboard's events and the GitHub trigger (push, pull_request) in slacronym's run record. A query across both repositories' records reads two meanings under one key.
  • GRS 3.2 item 036 and STIG V-222621 both set 30 months.
  • The verify-evidence.js citation of GRS 2.7 for audit events points at the employee health and safety schedule. Its "DAA-0567" for the permanent class was searched for in the GRS 5.1 text; "0567" does not occur there. GRS 5.1 is "Common Office Records".
  • The 90-day artifact retention in all three repositories is shorter than GRS 3.2 item 036's 30 months, GRS 3.1 item 020's 3 years, and STIG V-222621's 30 months; the S3 copies are the ones that could meet them.
  • The text extraction of SP 800-53A drops the "ti" and "fi" ligatures ("informaon"); quotations above restore the letters as printed. The extraction of GRS 3.2 interleaves its columns; item numbers and authorities were read from a layout extraction of pages 2 and 3.
  • The STIG file's CCI line for V-222622 (CCI-001872) carries an empty SP 800-53 Rev. 5 reference.

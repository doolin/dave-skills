Trusted timestamp (RFC 3161 time-stamp token)

A trusted timestamp is a time-stamp token: a structure signed by a time-stamping authority (TSA) that binds a hash of some data to the time the authority issued the token. Kept with the authority's certificate chain, it lets anyone with the data show that the data existed at that time. This page records the token's definition in RFC 3161 and RFC 5816, its place in a pipeline, the NIST, STIG, and Department of Defense and other federal documents that bear on it, and how one of the portfolio's three pipelines makes one. A token is evidence toward a control, and each control also requires a process; §3 states whose mapping each control is.

§1

What a time-stamp token is

factkindsource
The service: "A time-stamping service supports assertions of proof that a datum existed before a particular time." The authority "is a TTP that creates time-stamp tokens in order to indicate that a datum existed at a particular point in time."documentedR1 · §1, §2
Uses the RFC gives: to "verify that a digital signature was applied to a message before the corresponding certificate was revoked", to "indicate the time of submission when a deadline is critical, or to indicate the time of transaction for entries in a log."documentedR1 · §1
The authority sees a hash, not the data. It is required "to only time-stamp a hash representation of the datum, i.e., a data imprint associated with a one-way collision resistant hash-function uniquely identified by an OID", and "not to examine the imprint being time-stamped in any way (other than to check its length".documentedR1 · §2.1, items 6 and 8
The token carries no requester. The authority is required "not to include any identification of the requesting entity in the time-stamp tokens."documentedR1 · §2.1, item 9
The signing key: "The TSA MUST sign each time-stamp message with a key reserved specifically for that purpose." Its certificate carries one extended key usage, id-kp-timeStamping, and "This extension MUST be critical."documentedR1 · §2.3
The token's form: "It is defined as a ContentInfo ([CMS]) and SHALL encapsulate a signed data content type." Its content is the DER encoding of TSTInfo, and it "MUST NOT contain any signatures other than the signature of the TSA."documentedR1 · §2.4.2
RFC 5816 lets the signing certificate be identified by a hash other than SHA-1: the identifier is "either ESSCertID [ESS] or ESSCertIDv2 [ESSV2]", and "the SigningCertificateV2 attribute MUST be used if any algorithm other than SHA-1 is used".documentedR2 · §2.2.1

The fields of TSTInfo, with RFC 3161's description of each (R1 · §2.4.2).

fielddescription, verbatim
policy"The policy field MUST indicate the TSA's policy under which the response was produced."
messageImprint"MUST have the same value as the similar field in TimeStampReq". The request's imprint is "a hash algorithm OID and the hash value of the data to be time-stamped".
serialNumber"an integer assigned by the TSA to each TimeStampToken. It MUST be unique for each TimeStampToken issued by a given TSA".
genTime"the time at which the time-stamp token has been created by the TSA. It is expressed as UTC time".
accuracy"the time deviation around the UTC time contained in GeneralizedTime." Optional; "When the accuracy optional field is not present, then the accuracy may be available through other means, e.g., the TSAPolicyId."
orderingIf absent or false, "the genTime field only indicates the time at which the time-stamp token has been created by the TSA." Two tokens can then be ordered only when their genTimes differ by more than "the sum of the accuracies".
nonce"MUST be present if the similar field was present in TimeStampReq. In that case it MUST have the same value."
tsa"to give a hint in identifying the name of the TSA." Identification itself is by the certificate identifier in the signer information.
The request. A TimeStampReq carries the version, the messageImprint, and optionally a requested policy, a nonce, certReq, and extensions. "If the certReq field is present and set to true, the TSA's public key certificate ... MUST be provided by the TSA in the certificates field from the SignedData structure in that response." Over HTTP the request is sent as application/timestamp-query (R1 · §2.4.1, §3.4; R2 · §2.1).
What the requester verifies. On receipt the requester "SHALL verify the status error returned in the response and if no error is present it SHALL verify the various fields contained in the TimeStampToken and the validity of the digital signature", including "the correct certificate identifier of the TSA, the correct data imprint and the correct hash algorithm OID." It "SHALL then verify the timeliness of the response" against "a local trusted time reference" or the nonce. Then "the status of the certificate SHOULD be checked", and the client "SHOULD check the policy field" (R1 · §2.2).

§2

Where in a pipeline it is made

factkindsource
SP 800-204D asks for "the concurrent generation of evidence for build attestation to demonstrate compliance with secure build processes during the time of software delivery." The evidence includes "the hash of the final build artifact, files, libraries, and other materials used in the artifacts and all events", which "is then signed by a trusted component of the build framework that is not under the control of the developers".documentedS4 · §5.1.1
For signed evidence and its storage: "The attestations must be cryptographically signed using a secure key" and "The storage location must be tamper-proof and protected using robust access control."documentedS4 · §5.1.1
SP 800-204D's text contains no occurrence of "time-stamp", "timestamp", or "time stamp"; a third-party time record is not among its listed evidence (search of the full text, 2026-11-14).observedS4 · full text
SLSA's Build track recommends time-stamping as an alternative to a transparency log when signing provenance: build platforms should "use signing methodologies which improve the ability to detect and remediate key compromise, such as methods which rely on transparency logs or, when transparency isn’t appropriate, time stamping services."documentedL1 · Provenance is Authentic
SLSA provenance's own time fields are the builder's: startedOn, "The timestamp of when the build started", and finishedOn, "The timestamp of when the build completed." Both are optional.documentedL2 · BuildMetadata
The in-toto Statement layer v1 has no time field; "time" does not occur in it, nor "timestamp" in the framework's specification README (search, 2026-11-14).observedI1, I2 · full text
The stage, on this page's reading. A token can only follow the bytes it covers: it is requested after the evidence is collected and hashed, and before the evidence is archived. In springboard (§7) that is the last job of the run, after every evidence job and the evidence manifest.

§4

STIG

No rule in the Application Security and Development STIG V6R5 calls for a third-party time-stamp token. A search of its text for "3161", "time-stamping authority", "time stamping", and "TSA" found none. The rules that use "time stamp" concern the application's own audit-record clock (AU-8); the non-repudiation rule asks for digital signatures (AU-10). They are listed because a reviewer reading a token will be asked about them (T1).

ruleseveritytitle and check, verbatimCCI and SP 800-53 reference

Check: "If the application is required to provide non-repudiation services and does not, or if the non-repudiation functionality fails on demonstration, this is a finding." Two clocks. V-222497's sentence "externally generated time stamps may not be accurate" is about audit records the application writes. A time-stamp token is the reverse case: a time generated outside the system on purpose, so that the system's own clock is not the only witness. The STIG says nothing on that case.

§6

Limits stated in the sources

factkindsource
The RFC sets no operating requirements: "This standard does not establish overall security requirements for TSA operation". A TSA "will make known to prospective clients the policies it implements to ensure accurate time-stamp generation, and clients will make use of the services of a TSA only if they are satisfied that these policies meet their needs."documentedR1 · §1
Key compromise: when the authority's key is compromised, "Any token signed by the TSA using that private key cannot be trusted anymore." The RFC's remedies are an audit trail of all tokens and "Two time-stamp tokens from two different TSAs".documentedR1 · §4, item 2
Key lifetime: "the key will have a finite lifetime. Thus, any token signed by the TSA SHOULD be time-stamped again (if authentic copies of old CRLs are available) or notarized (if they aren't) at a later date to renew the trust that exists in the TSA's signature."documentedR1 · §4, item 3
Revocation with no reason code: "then all the tokens that have been signed with the corresponding key SHALL be considered as invalid."documentedR1 · §4, item 1
Replay: "Using a nonce always allows to detect replays, and hence its use is RECOMMENDED." Without one, the requester keeps "a local clock and a moving time window".documentedR1 · §4, item 6
Linkability: tokens over the same data "will include identical message imprints; as a result, an observer with access to those time-stamp tokens could infer that the time-stamps may refer to the same underlying data."documentedR1 · §4, item 5
Identity: the token holds no requester identity (§2.1 item 9), and "The time-stamp request does not identify the requester, as this information is not validated by the TSA". A token says when, not who.documentedR1 · §2.4.1
RFC 5816's change is for hash migration, not a new threat: it "is motivated by reasons of interoperability and migration to other hash algorithms rather than mitigating new security issues."documentedR2 · §3

§7

The portfolio's use

Of the three pipelines, only springboard takes an RFC 3161 timestamp. clubstraylight.com and slacronym take none.

factkindsource
Job attest ("Attest CI Artifacts") runs on a push to master after the reusable Golden Pipeline job compliance. It downloads every artifact of the run into ci-artifacts/ (actions/download-artifact v8.0.1, pinned by SHA, merge-multiple: true) and runs node scripts/attest.mjs in the step "Attest CI artifacts on Solana". TSA_URL is not set, so the default applies.portfoliospringboard · .github/workflows/ci-cd.yml · b2fdcb9
What is hashed: the script zips the regular files of ci-artifacts/ into ci-artifacts.zip and takes the SHA-256 of the zip. The query is built by openssl ts -query -digest <sha256 hex> -sha256 -cert -no_nonce: the imprint is the zip's hash, a certificate is requested, and no nonce is sent.portfoliospringboard · scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
The query is sent by HTTP POST, Content-Type: application/timestamp-query, to https://timestamp.sigstore.dev/api/v1/timestamp, the default (DEFAULT_TSA_URL), overridable by TSA_URL. The response is written to timestamp.tsr.portfoliospringboard · scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
The chain is fetched from the same service: the script replaces a trailing /timestamp in the URL with /timestamp/certchain and writes the reply to tsa-certchain.pem.portfoliospringboard · scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
Verification: openssl ts -verify -in timestamp.tsr -data ci-artifacts.zip -CAfile tsa-certchain.pem. The result is recorded as tsaVerified and as a step "TSA verification: PASSED" or "FAILED - …". A failure anywhere in the timestamp steps is logged as non-fatal and the run continues.portfoliospringboard · scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
The PDF report attestation.pdf has a section "Trusted Timestamp (RFC 3161)" with the TSA URL, a "Timestamp:" line, "Verified: yes" or "no", and the command openssl ts -verify -in timestamp.tsr -data ci-artifacts.zip -CAfile tsa-certchain.pem for a reader to rerun. attestation.json carries the same fields.portfoliospringboard · scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
Where it goes: the step "Upload attestation artifacts" (actions/upload-artifact v7.0.1, pinned by SHA, if: always()) uploads ci-artifacts.zip, attestation.pdf, attestation.json, timestamp.tsr, tsa-certchain.pem, and s3-receipt.json as the artifact attestation, retention 90 days. When S3_COMPLIANCE_BUCKET is set, the script also copies both timestamp files to s3://<bucket>/<root>/<yyyy/mm/dd>/<hhmmss>-<short commit>/, where the root is EVIDENCE_BUNDLE or else <repo>/ci, and lists them in s3-receipt.json.portfoliospringboard · .github/workflows/ci-cd.yml, scripts/attest.mjs · b2fdcb9
The evidence manifest's retention classes (scripts/verify-evidence.js) list no pattern for timestamp.tsr or tsa-certchain.pem; the manifest is made in the Golden Pipeline, before the timestamp exists.portfoliospringboard · scripts/verify-evidence.js · b2fdcb9
clubstraylight.com: ci/attest.mjs has no TSA step, and the attest job uploads attestation.pdf and attestation.json with no timestamp files. The script is "Copied from dave-skills skills/solana-cicd-hash/scripts/attest.mjs".portfolioclubstraylight.com · ci/attest.mjs, .github/workflows/ci.yml · b9235b2
slacronym: scripts/attest.mjs has no TSA step; the job uploads the attestation artifact with no timestamp files.portfolioslacronym · scripts/attest.mjs, .github/workflows/ci.yml · a9fb65d
The shared pattern has no RFC 3161 step: neither skills/solana-cicd-hash/ nor skills/cicd-golden-pipeline/ contains "TSA", "3161", "certchain", or "sigstore". springboard's timestamp is its own addition.portfoliodave-skills · skills/solana-cicd-hash, skills/cicd-golden-pipeline · 96d1f33
Two time records over one hash. springboard's script writes the same SHA-256 into two records. The Solana memo carries artifact_checksum and a timestamp field whose value is completedAt, the runner's clock, inside a transaction signed with the project's own keypair; it is recorded on a public ledger. The RFC 3161 token carries the hash and the authority's genTime, signed with a key whose certificate is reserved for time-stamping. The first time is asserted by the pipeline; the second is asserted by a third party. In clubstraylight.com and slacronym the memo is the attest script's only time record. The memo is described on attestation.html.

§8

Relationships

  • Evidence manifest. The token covers the zip, and the zip holds whatever evidence the run produced, the evidence manifest among it. The manifest lists each file's hash; the token dates the bundle (evidence-manifest.html).
  • Attestation and the Solana anchor. The PDF report carries the token's verification command beside the memo signature (§7, attestation.html).
  • Provenance. SLSA provenance carries the builder's own startedOn and finishedOn; SLSA recommends a transparency log or a time-stamping service for its signature (§2, provenance.html).
  • Audit events. AU-8 and the STIG's time-stamp rules govern the runner-clock times in the audit events; the token is a time from outside that clock (§4, audit-events.html).
  • OSCAL. An assessment result that cites the evidence can cite the token as the record of when the evidence existed (oscal.html).
  • SBOM, scans, tests, coverage. In springboard the SBOM, the secrets scan, vulnerability scan, static analysis, test results, and coverage are run artifacts; those that land as top-level files in ci-artifacts/ are in the zip the token covers (§9). SR-4 and the PS.3 tasks are shared with the SBOM page (sbom.html §6).

§9

Observed

  • The "Timestamp:" line of springboard's PDF and the tsaTimestamp field of attestation.json are set by new Date().toISOString() after verification: the runner's clock, not the token's genTime. The authority's time is only in timestamp.tsr (reading of scripts/attest.mjs at b2fdcb9, 2026-11-14).
  • The request has no nonce (-no_nonce), and the script does not compare genTime with the runner's clock. Of the two timeliness checks in RFC 3161 §2.2, neither is made. Nor does the script check the policy field against an expected value, or the authority certificate's revocation status beyond what openssl ts -verify does by default (2026-11-14).
  • The trust anchor for verification is the chain the authority served in the same run. A pass shows the token is consistent with that chain; it does not show the chain is Sigstore's. Pinning or separately obtaining the chain is not done (2026-11-14).
  • The token covers ci-artifacts.zip only. attestation.pdf, attestation.json, and s3-receipt.json are written after the request and are not covered by it (2026-11-14).
  • The chain URL is derived by text substitution. A TSA_URL that does not end in /timestamp is left unchanged, so the chain is requested by GET from the timestamp endpoint itself; if that fails, the step is logged as non-fatal with timestamp.tsr already written and unverified (reading of fetchTsaCertChain, 2026-11-14).
  • The zip takes only the regular files at the top of ci-artifacts/; a subdirectory left by an artifact uploaded as a directory is skipped, and so is not covered by the token (reading of resolveArtifactFiles, 2026-11-14).
  • The attest job depends on compliance only, not on package, so whether the packaged JAR's artifact is in ci-artifacts/ when it is downloaded depends on which job finishes first (reading of ci-cd.yml, 2026-11-14).
  • The SP 800-53A text extraction drops the "ti" ligature ("mestamps" for "timestamps"); quotations in §3 restore it from the PDF's wording.
  • The comment at the head of ci-cd.yml says "main push"; the job condition is refs/heads/master.

Gaps and unverified claims

  • Sigstore's own TSA documentation is not on disk: its policy OID, the accuracy and ordering it sets, the certificate chain it serves, its signing key's custody and lifetime, and whether it keeps a log of issued tokens. None was read.
  • No timestamp.tsr from a springboard run was read, so the token's actual genTime, policy, serial number, and ESSCertID version are not observed.
  • OpenSSL's manual for openssl ts is not on disk. That -cert sets certReq, and what -verify checks by default (revocation, policy, EKU), are unverified.
  • How Solana records the time of a block or transaction is not in the memo program page on disk; the contrast in §7 rests on the memo's own timestamp field.
  • Whether actions/attest-build-provenance, used by springboard's package job, obtains a time-stamp or a transparency-log entry: the GitHub page on disk has no occurrence of "timestamp", "transparency", "Rekor", or "Sigstore".
  • RFC 3161 §2.2 requires both an imprint check and a timeliness check; whether an assessor would accept a token obtained without a nonce or a clock comparison is not stated in any source read.
  • ETSI's TSA policy and profile standards, and RFC 5652 (CMS), RFC 5035 (ESSCertIDv2), were not read.
  • The SSDF 1.2 draft's reference column interleaves with its task column in the extracted text; the 1.2 mappings in §3 were read from that text and not checked against the PDF's layout.
  • The DISA CCI list was not read separately; the CCI-to-control references in §4 are those printed in the STIG.
